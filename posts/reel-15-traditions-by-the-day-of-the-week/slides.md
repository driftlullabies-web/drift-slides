## ⚠️ CTA IS BEN'S OWN, 2026-09-04 — and it deliberately does NOT match the Keepsake reels

> *"the traditions cta should feel different. the eyebrow is more of a transition to the cta like it
> was previously."* — then, verbatim: eyebrow *"any lullaby can become something special"*, ask
> *"but a song made just for **them** will unlock feelings you never knew you had"*.

**Why the difference is right, not an inconsistency.** REEL 13 and REEL 14 are loss- and gain-framed
KEEPSAKE cards: their items create memory FOMO, so their eyebrow has to answer it (`§8.7` — memory
attaches to music). This card is **aspirational** — a list of traditions to start. There is no
memory being lost, so a memory-mechanism eyebrow would be answering a question the card never asked.

So this one goes back to the classic locked shape: **the eyebrow concedes** (any lullaby works) and
**the ask turns** (but one made for them does something more). Same move as REEL 12's "Any lullaby
can work." / "But imagine one written just for your little one" — the pattern this lane already had.

⚠️ **Note on the claim, for the record, not as an objection.** *"will unlock feelings you never knew
you had"* is an unhedged promise, where the Keepsake eyebrows are all hedged with `can`. That is a
real distinction and it holds up: [[drift-claim-softening]] governs **physiological and science**
claims ("music slows their heart rate", "memory attaches to music") and universal absolutes. This is
a claim about how a song will make *her* feel — closer to a promise about the product's effect than
an assertion about how brains work. Left exactly as written. If the line ever needs softening it is
for tone, not for the claim rule.

Ask renders at 40px over two lines (vs 44px for the shorter Keepsake ask) — still comfortably the
dominant element over 34px items. `*them*` renders italic lavender per `§8.8`.
