## Body
2 BEFORE SHE WAS BORN — I wrote her a lullaby.
3 NEWBORN — I had no idea how to get my newborn to sleep. / Bouncing. The rocking chair. Rocking while standing. Swaddles. Sleep sacks. The mobile. / The song was one more thing to try.
4 But it gave me something I needed: a routine. / Something I knew how to do. / A way to give her my love.
5 6–12 MONTHS — It became her cue that it was time to sleep. / Sometimes she'd get sad when she heard it. She knew the crib was coming.
6 1 YEAR — We taught it to her grandparents. At her first sleepovers, they could do bedtime without us. / On vacations, it was a piece of home. We sang it extra.
7 18 MONTHS — It got stuck in her head. / She'd babble bits of it while she played.
8 2 YEARS — She asked for her song at bedtime, and in the middle of the night. / So many times, it took her from upset to calm. / That year we moved, and it was a hard adjustment. The song came with us.
9 3 YEARS — She sang and danced all day. But at bedtime, she still just wanted to listen. / She started singing it to her stuffies when she put them to bed.
10 4 YEARS — She was a sleeping pro. Some nights we sang it really quickly. She didn't need it anymore. / But after a scary dream, the very first thing she asked for was her lullaby. / It worked almost every time.
11 5 YEARS — Then one night: / "Please stop singing, Daddy." / She wanted the spotlight. / The sun will rise in morning, the stars will shine at night. I will always love you, while you're looking for your light.
12 I teared up. I just looked at her with wonder. / Hearing her voice sing her lullaby, the memories came flooding back. / Holding her as a newborn. Every new age. Every home and every bedroom where we sang that song.

## Closer
That's when I decided to help other families write a lullaby for their own little ones.
